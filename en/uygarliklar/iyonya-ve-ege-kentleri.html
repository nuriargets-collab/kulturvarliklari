Ionia and the ancient cities of the Aegean

Civilizations and periods › Ionia and the ancient cities of the Aegean

With cities like Ephesus, Miletus and Priene, Ionia was one of the birthplaces of philosophy, science and town planning.

Founded along the coast between İzmir and Didim, the Ionian cities were among the liveliest centres of trade and thought in the Mediterranean in the 7th–6th centuries BC. The first philosophers to explain nature through reason, Thales, Anaximander and Anaximenes, came from Miletus. Grid planning, attributed to Hippodamus of Miletus, can still be traced in the orderly streets of Priene.

In Roman times Ephesus became one of the largest cities of the province of Asia. The Library of Celsus, the great theatre, the mosaics and frescoes of the Terrace Houses and the remains of the Temple of Artemis, one of the Seven Wonders, make it one of Türkiye's most visited sites; Ephesus has been on the UNESCO World Heritage List since 2015. The Basilica of St John on Ayasuluk Hill shows the city's importance in the Christian era too.

The Temple of Apollo at Didyma was one of the greatest oracle centres of the ancient world. Teos, Klazomenai, Klaros and İzmir's Agora complete the route, and the Ephesus Museum, Miletus Museum and İzmir Archaeology Museum are the best places to see the finds together.
