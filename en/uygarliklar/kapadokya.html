Cappadocia: fairy chimneys, rock churches and underground cities

Civilizations and periods › Cappadocia: fairy chimneys, rock churches and underground cities
Period Volcanic origin · settled from the Bronze Age to todayPlaces 18Reading 1 min🔊 Audio🎓 Take the quiz

Cappadocia: fairy chimneys, rock churches and underground cities. In Cappadocia, shaped by volcanoes and wind, people carved their lives into the soft rock for thousands of years. Ash from volcanoes such as Erciyes, Hasan Dağı and Göllüdağ formed a layer of soft tuff. Worn by wind and rain, it produced fairy chimneys topped with hard rock 'caps'. The easily carved rock let people cut out homes, dovecotes, storerooms and churches. In the Byzantine period the region became an important monastic centre. The rock churches of the Göreme Open-Air Museum are famous for their 10th–12th-century frescoes; the Dark Church preserves its colours best. Churches in the Ihlara Valley, Soğanlı, Gümüşler and Gülşehir show other sides of this tradition. Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of Cappadocia joined the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1985. Underground cities such as Derinkuyu, Kaymaklı, Özkonak and Mazı are multi-level complexes where thousands could shelter in times of danger, with huge rolling stones to seal passages, ventilation shafts and wine presses. The fairy chimneys of Zelve and Paşabağları, the Roman mosaics of Sobesos and the Nevşehir and Ürgüp museums complete the region.
