Caria

Civilizations and periods › Caria

Reaching from Bodrum into inland Muğla, Caria is home to the Mausoleum, one of the Seven Wonders of the ancient world.

The Carians lived in a region covering today's Muğla and part of Aydın and spoke their own language. Caria's golden age came in the 4th century BC under the Hecatomnid dynasty, who governed the region for the Persian Empire. The dynasty's first capital was Mylasa (Milas), where the Uzunyuva monumental tomb is thought to belong to Hecatomnus.

King Mausolus moved the capital to Halicarnassus (Bodrum). The splendid tomb built for him after his death was counted among the Seven Wonders, and the word 'mausoleum' comes from it. Its foundations and fragments can be seen today at the Mausoleum Museum in Bodrum. The historian Herodotus was also from Halicarnassus.

The sanctuary of Zeus at Labraunda, the temple of Hecate at Lagina linked by a sacred way to Stratonikeia, the rock tombs of Kaunos and UNESCO-listed Aphrodisias, famed for its school of sculpture, complete the picture. The Bodrum Museum of Underwater Archaeology tells of the region's history bound up with the sea.
