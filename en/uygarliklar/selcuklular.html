The Seljuks and the Beyliks

Civilizations and periods › The Seljuks and the Beyliks
Period 1071 – 15th centuryPlaces 14Reading 1 min🔊 Audio🎓 Take the quiz

The Seljuks and the Beyliks. Settling in Anatolia after Manzikert, the Turks made Konya their capital and built a new culture of caravanserais, madrasas and tile art. Sultan Alp Arslan's victory over the Byzantine emperor Romanos Diogenes at Manzikert (Malazgirt) in 1071 is seen as the turning point of Turkish settlement in Anatolia. The Anatolian Seljuk state, with its capital at Konya, flourished in the 12th–13th centuries. Caravanserais built along the trade routes show how much the state valued commerce and travellers. The tiles of the Karatay Madrasa and the Mevlana Museum in Konya are symbols of Seljuk art and the Sufi tradition; the thought of Mevlana Jalaluddin Rumi, who lived in the 13th century, is read all over the world today. The Seljuk Cemetery at Ahlat, with its carved gravestones, is a unique open-air museum, and Alanya Castle reflects Seljuk power on the Mediterranean. The Great Mosque and Hospital of Divriği in Sivas have been on the UNESCO list since 1985. As the Seljuk state broke up, the era of the Anatolian beyliks began. Kütahya of the Germiyanids, with its tile-making, and Hasankeyf, which flourished under the Artuqids on the Tigris, represent this period. The complex at Hacıbektaş shows the diversity of Anatolia's spiritual world.
