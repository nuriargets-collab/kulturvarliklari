The Kingdom of Commagene

Civilizations and periods › The Kingdom of Commagene
Period 2nd c. BC – AD 72Places 8Reading 1 min🔊 Audio🎓 Take the quiz

The Kingdom of Commagene. The small but ambitious Kingdom of Commagene on the Euphrates tried to unite East and West, even in its gods. Commagene was a small kingdom around today's Adıyaman that gained independence as the Seleucid Empire weakened. Its rulers traced their lineage both to the Persian kings and to Alexander the Great. Its most famous king, Antiochus I, had a huge tumulus and ceremonial terraces built for himself on the summit of Mount Nemrut in the 1st century BC. In Nemrut's colossal statues, gods such as Zeus-Oromasdes, Apollo-Mithras and Heracles-Artagnes bear both Greek and Persian names, and the king is shown shaking hands with the gods. The site joined the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1987. Sunrise and sunset are the times to see the stone faces at their most striking. Arsameia (Eski Kahta), sanctuary of Antiochus's father Mithridates, the Karakuş tumulus of the royal women of Commagene, the Roman-era Cendere Bridge and Kahta Yenikale lie on the same route. The kingdom joined Rome in AD 72; the mosaics of its important city Zeugma are displayed at the Zeugma Mosaic Museum in Gaziantep.
