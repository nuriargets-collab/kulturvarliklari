Phrygians and Lydians

Civilizations and periods › Phrygians and Lydians
Period 9th–6th century BC · Iron AgePlaces 8Reading 1 min🔊 Audio🎓 Take the quiz

Phrygians and Lydians. Two great kingdoms of post-Hittite Anatolia: King Midas's Phrygia and Lydia, which struck the first coins. The Phrygians built a powerful kingdom in central and western Anatolia in the 9th–7th centuries BC. Their capital Gordion lies in Polatlı, Ankara; its great tumulus is thought to belong to the family of the famous King Midas. Gordion joined the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2023. Rock-cut façades such as the Midas Monument (Yazılıkaya) in Eskişehir and the cult of the mother goddess Cybele at Pessinus are symbols of the Phrygian world. The Kingdom of Lydia, with its capital at Sardis (Salihli, Manisa), dominated western Anatolia in the 7th–6th centuries BC. The Lydians are known as the first people to strike coins, their wealth coming from the electrum-bearing sands of the Pactolus. The riches of the last king, Croesus, became proverbial; the kingdom fell to the Persians in 546 BC. The Gordion Museum, Midas Yazılıkaya and the Museum of Anatolian Civilizations in Ankara tell the Phrygian story, while the site of Sardis, the Manisa Museum and the Uşak Museum, home of the 'Croesus Treasure' that was smuggled abroad and later returned, tell Lydia's.
