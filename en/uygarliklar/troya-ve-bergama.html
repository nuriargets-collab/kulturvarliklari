Troy and Pergamon

Civilizations and periods › Troy and Pergamon

A journey through the northern Aegean, from the Troy of legend to Pergamon, one of the most brilliant kingdoms of the Hellenistic world.

Troy in Çanakkale is made up of layers of cities built one on top of another over about 4,000 years. Identified with the Trojan War described in Homer's Iliad, the site joined the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1998. The Troy Museum next to the site presents these layers and the region's finds in a modern way. Assos, where Aristotle lived for a time, and Alexandria Troas are other ancient cities worth seeing.

Pergamon (Bergama) was a capital that shone in the 3rd–2nd centuries BC among the Hellenistic kingdoms founded after Alexander the Great. Its acropolis, with a theatre set into a steep slope, the Temple of Trajan and a rich library, is a masterpiece of Hellenistic town planning. The word 'parchment' is said to come from Pergamon's name. The reliefs of the Altar of Zeus are today in Berlin.

The Asklepion of Pergamon, where the physician Galen also worked, was one of the ancient world's famous healing centres, and the Red Basilica is a huge Roman-era temple. Pergamon joined the UNESCO list in 2014 as the 'Pergamon and its Multi-Layered Cultural Landscape'.
