Lycia

Civilizations and periods › Lycia
Period 1st millennium BC – Roman periodPlaces 15Reading 1 min🔊 Audio🎓 Take the quiz

Lycia. On the Teke Peninsula, where mountains meet the sea, lived the Lycians, known for their rock-cut tombs and a league of their own. The Lycians lived along the mountainous coast between today's Fethiye and Antalya and had their own language and script. Their most striking legacy is tombs carved into sheer rock with house- and temple-like façades, and pillar tombs raised on stone shafts. The Amyntas rock tomb in Fethiye and the rock necropolises of Tlos, Pinara and Myra are among the finest examples. Lycian cities joined in the Lycian League, governed by delegates whose votes depended on each city's size; the council house at Patara was its meeting place. Xanthos and its sanctuary, the Letoon, are the heart of Lycia and have been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1988; a trilingual inscription from the Letoon helped scholars understand the Lycian language. The region remained important in Roman and Byzantine times; Myra is linked to Saint Nicholas, the origin of the Santa Claus tradition. The Museum of Lycian Civilizations in Demre, Arykanda, Limyra, Olympos, Phaselis and Simena with the sunken ruins of Kekova make a route that pairs well with hiking the Lycian Way.
