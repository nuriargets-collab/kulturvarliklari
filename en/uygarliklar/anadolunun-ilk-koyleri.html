Anatolia's first villages: Aşıklı Höyük and Çatalhöyük

Civilizations and periods › Anatolia's first villages: Aşıklı Höyük and Çatalhöyük

Anatolia's first villages: Aşıklı Höyük and Çatalhöyük. Central Anatolia offers some of the world's best-known examples of the shift from hunting and gathering to farming. At Aşıklı Höyük in Aksaray, a village of mud-brick houses was founded about ten thousand years ago, and its people took early steps in domesticating sheep and goats. A little later, Çatalhöyük on the Konya Plain grew into a large settlement home to thousands of people. At Çatalhöyük the houses stood wall to wall; there were no streets, and people entered through openings in the roofs. Wall paintings, reliefs and burials beneath house floors show how daily life and belief were intertwined. The site was added to the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2012. The Museum of Anatolian Civilizations in Ankara holds one of the richest collections of this period, including Çatalhöyük's wall paintings and its famous seated woman figurine. The Konya Archaeology Museum and Aksaray Museum also show finds from the region, and Karain Cave near Antalya preserves traces of even earlier hunter-gatherers.
