The Ottoman Empire

Civilizations and periods › The Ottoman Empire

The Ottoman Empire. Born as a small principality in Söğüt, the Ottoman state grew into an empire spanning three continents and more than six centuries. The Ottoman principality emerged in the late 13th century around Söğüt in Bilecik. Bursa (1326) and Edirne were the first capitals; the complexes of Bursa and the Nilüfer Hatun Imaret in İznik are fine examples of early Ottoman architecture. Bursa and Cumalıkızık joined the UNESCO list in 2014 as 'the Birth of the Ottoman Empire'. Rumeli Hisarı, built in 1452 at the narrowest point of the Bosphorus, heralded Mehmed II's conquest of Istanbul the following year. The empire peaked in the 16th century under Süleyman the Magnificent and the architect Sinan; the Selimiye Mosque and Complex in Edirne, which Sinan called his masterpiece, has been on the UNESCO list since 2011. The Museum of Turkish and Islamic Arts and the Galata Mevlevi Lodge in Istanbul show the richness of this culture. The Ottoman legacy lives not only in palaces but in the fabric of towns: the timber mansions of UNESCO-listed Safranbolu, the Çakırağa Mansion in Birgi, the Hazeranlar Mansion in Amasya and the Latifoğlu Mansion in Tokat show daily life, while İshak Paşa Palace in Doğubayazıt shows splendour at the empire's eastern edge.
