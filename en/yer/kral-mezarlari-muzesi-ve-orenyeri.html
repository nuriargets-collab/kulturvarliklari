The Royal Tombs, west of ancient Salamis, are the city's elite necropolis of the 8th–7th centuries BC. Their wide stone-lined approach ramps (dromoi) and monumental chambers yielded skeletons of horses sacrificed with the dead and remains of chariots – burial customs that recall the heroic funerals described by Homer. A small site museum presents the finds and rituals; a tomb reused in Roman times and later turned into a chapel, known locally as 'St Catherine's Prison', can also be visited.
