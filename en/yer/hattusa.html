Hattusa

Hattuşa

Hattusa was the capital of the Hittite Empire, a great Bronze Age power, and for some five hundred years governed a state stretching from Anatolia to Syria. Kilometres of walls are pierced by monumental gates – the Lion Gate, King's Gate and the sphinx-guarded Yerkapı, whose stone tunnel is a masterpiece of Hittite engineering. Tens of thousands of cuneiform tablets found here illuminate Hittite history, religion and diplomacy, including the Hittite copy of the Treaty of Kadesh with Egypt, one of the oldest known peace treaties. The rock reliefs of nearby Yazılıkaya reveal the Hittite pantheon. UNESCO World Heritage since 1986.

Visitor guide

🚌 Getting there

Hattusa is at Boğazkale, about 80 km southwest of Çorum. Minibuses run from Çorum via Sungurlu to Boğazkale, but a car is very useful for the 6 km circuit road. The Yazılıkaya sanctuary is about 2 km from the site.

⭐ Don't miss

  • The Lion Gate, the King's Gate and the 70-metre stone tunnel at Yerkapı
  • The Great Temple and the palace area on Büyükkale
  • The reconstructed section of the city wall
  • The procession of gods at Yazılıkaya
  • The Boğazköy Museum

🏺 Finds and artworks

  • Hattusa's cuneiform tablets and original parts of the Lion Gate are in the Museum of Anatolian Civilizations in Ankara
  • One tablet of the Treaty of Kadesh is in the Istanbul Archaeology Museums

👤 Notable people

  • Capital of the Hittite Empire
  • King Hattusili III and Queen Puduhepa, who concluded the Treaty of Kadesh, ruled here

💡 Tips

The site is huge; driving the circuit road and walking from stop to stop is the most efficient way. Add Yazılıkaya and the Boğazköy Museum to the same day; with Alacahöyük you complete the Hittite route.
