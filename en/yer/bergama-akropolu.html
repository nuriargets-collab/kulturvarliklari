Acropolis of Pergamon

The Acropolis of Bergama was the political and cultural heart of Pergamon, capital of the Hellenistic Kingdom of Pergamon, built on a steep hill rising hundreds of metres above the plain. Under the Attalids the city rivalled Alexandria in art and learning, remembered for its library and parchment. Its most striking structure is the theatre cut into the slope, one of the steepest in the ancient world, while the white marble columns of the Roman Temple of Trajan define the skyline. The friezes of the famous Altar of Zeus are now in Berlin. Reached by cable car; UNESCO World Heritage since 2014.

🎧 A day in Pergamon

It is 170 BC, in the reign of King Eumenes II. You climb the road that winds up from the plain. Every terrace holds something new: a market, sports grounds, temples.

Near the top the Altar of Zeus comes into view, its marble frieze full of gods fighting giants. A little further on is the library, where scholars read and copy works written on parchment.

In the late afternoon you go down to the theatre. The rows are so steep that from the top the stage looks the size of your palm. You watch the play with ten thousand others while the plain stretches out below.

Today only the altar's foundation remains on the hill; its frieze is in a museum in Berlin. But the theatre still clings to the same slope, and a cable car now carries visitors to the top.

Visitor guide

🚌 Getting there

The Acropolis of Pergamon stands on the hill right above the town of Bergama. A cable car from the station in town takes you up in a few minutes; you can also drive to the car park on top. Buses run regularly from İzmir to Bergama.

⭐ Don't miss

  • One of the steepest ancient theatres in the world
  • The white marble columns of the Temple of Trajan
  • Remains of the Library of Pergamon
  • The foundations of the Altar of Zeus
  • Views over the Bakırçay plain and the Red Basilica

🏺 Finds and artworks

  • The Altar of Zeus itself was taken to Germany in the 19th century and is in the Pergamon Museum in Berlin
  • The Bergama Museum shows a model of the altar and sculpture from the city

👤 Notable people

  • Capital of the Attalid kings
  • Parchment ("pergamena") takes its name from the city
  • The physician Galen was born here

💡 Tips

The theatre steps are very steep; take care going down and wear non-slip shoes. Start early to see the Acropolis, the Asklepieion and the Red Basilica in one day; ask at the ticket office about combined tickets.
