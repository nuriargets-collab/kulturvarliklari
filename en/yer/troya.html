Troy became the most famous city in world literature through Homer's Iliad. The mound of Hisarlık holds nine main layers built one upon another over some four thousand years, from the Early Bronze Age to Roman times. Heinrich Schliemann's excavations in the 1870s showed the world that the legend rested on a real place, though his hasty methods also damaged some layers. Later scientific work revealed a powerful city controlling trade between the Aegean and the Black Sea. Visitors walk among walls, ramps, megarons and a Roman theatre. UNESCO World Heritage since 1998.

🎧 A day in Troy

It is 1300 BC. Your ship, coming in from the Aegean with the wind behind it, sails into the bay. On the hill, behind stone walls and towers, rises the citadel; perhaps this is the city Homer called well-walled.

Walking up from the harbour, you pass through the houses of the lower town. Merchants trade goods from the Aegean islands, from Anatolia and from the Black Sea.

In the late afternoon you enter through the citadel gate. From the walls both the strait and the bay lie at your feet. That is why everyone wants to take Troy.

Today the sea is about 5 km away; rivers filled the bay with silt and turned it into a plain. In the middle of the mound lies the deep trench Schliemann dug; in its walls you can see the layers of nine cities like slices of cake.

🚌 Getting there

Troy is about 30 km southwest of central Çanakkale, next to the village of Tevfikiye. Minibuses run from Çanakkale; there is parking at the entrance for drivers. The Troy Museum is about 1 km from the site.

⭐ Don't miss

  • The large wooden horse at the entrance square
  • Layers from Troy I to Troy IX built one over another
  • The walls and gates of Troy VI
  • Schliemann's great trench
  • Finds displayed layer by layer in the Troy Museum

🏺 Finds and artworks

  • The Polyxena Sarcophagus and finds displayed by layer in the Troy Museum
  • Most of Schliemann's "Treasure of Priam" is today in the Pushkin Museum in Moscow

👤 Notable people

  • Heinrich Schliemann began excavating in 1870
  • Known as the setting of Homer's Iliad

💡 Tips

Visiting the Troy Museum first makes the site much easier to understand. Follow the numbered signs along the paths. It can be windy, so bring a layer; combine it with Assos or Gallipoli.
