Knidos, at the tip of the Datça peninsula where the Aegean meets the Mediterranean, was a Dorian city with two harbours. It was famed for art and science: Praxiteles' Aphrodite, among the first great nude female statues, is said to have stood here, and the astronomer Eudoxus and Sostratus, architect of the Pharos of Alexandria, are known as Knidians. Theatres, temples, the round podium of the Aphrodite temple and the harbours terrace the slope; the sunsets are unforgettable.

🎧 A day in Knidos

The year is 300 BC. Ships shelter in one of the two harbours; whichever way the wind blows, the other is easy to enter. On the quay, wine amphorae are being loaded.

You climb the terraces to the round temple of Aphrodite. Everyone has come from distant cities to see Praxiteles' famous statue.

In the afternoon you are shown where an astronomer made his observations. Towards evening you glimpse, on a headland outside the city, the huge lion statue gazing out to sea.

Today the Aphrodite is lost and known only from copies. The Lion of Knidos was shipped to London in 1858 and is displayed in the British Museum.

Visitor guide

🚌 Getting there

Knidos is at Cape Tekir on the tip of the Datça peninsula in Muğla, about 35 km from Datça town. The road is narrow and winding; go by car, tour minibus or, in summer, by boat from Datça.

⭐ Don't miss

  • The two ancient harbours
  • The round foundation of the Temple of Aphrodite
  • The large and small theatres
  • The Doric stoa and terraces
  • The sundial
  • The view from the cape where the Aegean meets the Mediterranean

🏺 Finds and artworks

  • Praxiteles' famous Aphrodite of Knidos is lost and known only from Roman copies (the best known is in the Vatican Museums)
  • The Lion of Knidos and the Demeter statue, found in the 19th century, are in the British Museum in London

👤 Notable people

  • The astronomer and mathematician Eudoxus and Sostratus, architect of the Lighthouse of Alexandria, were from Knidos
  • Praxiteles' Aphrodite was displayed here

💡 Tips

Sunset from the cape is spectacular, but the drive back in the dark is demanding. If you come by boat you can anchor in the harbour. Bring water and a hat; there is little shade.
