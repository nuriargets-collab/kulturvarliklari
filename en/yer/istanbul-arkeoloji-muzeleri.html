Istanbul Archaeology Museums, in Gülhane in the outer gardens of Topkapı Palace, were founded as the Ottoman Empire's first modern museum. Developed under the painter and archaeologist Osman Hamdi Bey, the complex has three buildings: the neoclassical Archaeology Museum, the Museum of the Ancient Orient and the Tiled Kiosk from the reign of Mehmed the Conqueror. Its collection spans hundreds of thousands of objects from across the Ottoman world. The Alexander Sarcophagus and the Mourning Women Sarcophagus from the royal necropolis of Sidon are world-famous; the Ancient Orient section holds a tablet of the Treaty of Kadesh and glazed-brick reliefs from Babylon's Ishtar Gate. Easily a full day.

Visitor guide

🚌 Getting there

The Istanbul Archaeology Museums are in Sultanahmet, between the outer courtyard of Topkapı Palace and Gülhane Park. It is a few minutes' walk from the Gülhane stop on the T1 tram.

⭐ Don't miss

  • The Archaeology Museum building, the Museum of the Ancient Orient and the Tiled Kiosk
  • The sarcophagi from the royal necropolis of Sidon
  • Galleries on the history of Istanbul
  • Sarcophagi and capitals in the garden

🏺 Finds and artworks

  • The Alexander Sarcophagus and the Mourning Women Sarcophagus
  • A tablet of the Treaty of Kadesh
  • The Siloam Inscription
  • Glazed-brick lion panels from Babylon's Ishtar Gate

👤 Notable people

  • The museum was founded by the painter and archaeologist Osman Hamdi Bey, who also excavated the Sidon sarcophagi

💡 Tips

As it has three buildings, allow at least 2–3 hours. It is easy to combine with Topkapı Palace on the same day via the palace's outer courtyard.
