⭐ Star pieces

Masterpieces not to miss in Türkiye's museums: the story, date and museum of each.

Seated Woman of Çatalhöyük

c. 6000 BC

This small terracotta figurine shows a full-bodied woman resting her hands on two felines at her sides. It was found in a grain bin during the 1961 excavations at Çatalhöyük and is usually read as a symbol of fertility and power. It is one of the best-known works of Neolithic Anatolia.

Where: Anadolu Medeniyetleri Müzesi · Ankara

📷 Dosseman · CC BY-SA 4.0

Alacahöyük Sun Discs and Stag Statuettes

c. 2500–2200 BC

The bronze sun discs and stag statuettes from the princely tombs of Alacahöyük are masterpieces of Early Bronze Age Anatolian metalwork. They are thought to have been carried on poles in ceremonies. The sun disc long served as a symbol of Ankara and of Anatolia.

Where: Anadolu Medeniyetleri Müzesi · Ankara

📷 Dosseman · CC BY-SA 4.0

Urfa Man

c. 9000 BC

About 11,000 years old, this limestone statue is regarded as the oldest known life-size human sculpture. It was found during construction work in Şanlıurfa in 1993, and its obsidian eyes are still in place. It sheds light on the world of the Göbeklitepe era.

Where: Şanlıurfa Arkeoloji Müzesi · Şanlıurfa

📷 Immanuelle · CC BY-SA 4.0

Gypsy Girl Mosaic

Roman period

Recovered from Zeugma and famous for its gaze, this mosaic is displayed in a dim, dedicated room at the Zeugma Mosaic Museum. Fragments smuggled abroad after the excavations were returned from the United States in 2018, making the work whole again. It has become a symbol of Gaziantep.

Where: Zeugma Mozaik Müzesi · Gaziantep

📷 Dosseman · CC BY-SA 4.0

Alexander Sarcophagus

late 4th century BC

Found by Osman Hamdi Bey in 1887 in the royal necropolis of Sidon, this marble sarcophagus shows Alexander the Great in battle and hunting scenes, which give it its name; it was probably made for Abdalonymus, king of Sidon. Traces of the original paint can still be seen.

Where: İstanbul Arkeoloji Müzeleri · İstanbul

📷 Dosseman · CC BY-SA 4.0

Treaty of Kadesh Tablet

c. 1259 BC

This clay tablet from Hattusa is the Hittite copy of the treaty between King Hattusili III and Pharaoh Ramesses II of Egypt. It is counted among the oldest known written peace treaties, and a replica is displayed at the United Nations headquarters in New York.

Where: İstanbul Arkeoloji Müzeleri · İstanbul

📷 Osama Shukir Muhammed Amin FRCP(Glasg) · CC BY-SA 4.0

Siloam Inscription

late 8th century BC

This short Hebrew inscription records the cutting of a rock-hewn water tunnel in Jerusalem; it was found in 1880 and brought to Istanbul in the Ottoman period. It uniquely describes how the workers, digging from both ends, met in the middle. It is one of the oldest known Hebrew inscriptions.

Where: İstanbul Arkeoloji Müzeleri · İstanbul

📷 ywpark2003 (Prof. Yong Woo Park) · CC0

Weary Herakles

2nd century AD

The upper half of this Roman marble statue, found at Perge in 1980, was smuggled abroad and spent many years in a Boston museum. Returned to Türkiye in 2011, it was reunited with its lower half. The statue has become a symbol of the fight to repatriate cultural property.

Where: Antalya Müzesi · Antalya

📷 Carole Raddato · CC BY-SA 2.0

Artemis Statues of Ephesus

Roman period

The Artemis statues in the Ephesus Museum show the goddess in her distinctly Anatolian form, her chest covered with egg-shaped ornaments and her robe with lions and bulls. This image of the goddess worshipped at the Temple of Artemis, one of the Seven Wonders of the ancient world, is the emblem of Ephesus.

Where: Efes Müzesi · İzmir

📷 Dosseman · CC BY-SA 4.0

Lydian Hoard (Karun Treasure)

6th century BC

Looted from Lydian burial mounds near Uşak in the 1960s, this collection of gold, silver and jewellery was returned to Türkiye from the United States in 1993 after a long legal battle. It is named after the Lydian king Croesus (Karun in Turkish). The winged seahorse brooch is its most famous piece.

Where: Uşak Müzesi · Uşak

📷 Dosseman · CC BY-SA 4.0

Polyxena Sarcophagus

late 6th century BC

Found in a burial mound near Biga in 1994, this marble sarcophagus shows on one side the sacrifice of Polyxena, daughter of Priam, after the Trojan War, and a banquet scene on others. It is one of the oldest marble sarcophagi with figural scenes in Anatolia and a highlight of the Troy Museum.

Where: Troya Müzesi · Çanakkale

📷 Dosseman · CC BY-SA 4.0

Sebasteion Reliefs

1st century AD

The marble reliefs that adorned the Sebasteion, the complex dedicated to the Roman emperors at Aphrodisias, show emperors alongside gods and heroes, and conquered peoples as personifications. Most surviving panels are displayed in a large hall of the museum built for them. They reflect the mastery of the Aphrodisias school of sculpture.

Where: Afrodisias Müzesi ve Örenyeri · Aydın

📷 Dosseman · CC BY-SA 4.0

Antioch Mosaics: Oceanus and Tethys

2nd–3rd century AD

The Hatay Archaeology Museum holds one of the richest collections of Roman mosaics in the world. Among the mosaics from the villas of ancient Antioch and Daphne, a panel showing the sea god Oceanus and his consort Tethys among fish and sea creatures stands out. The mosaics reflect the city's wealth and daily life in Roman times.

Where: Hatay Arkeoloji Müzesi · Hatay

📷 Dosseman · CC BY-SA 4.0

Great Palace Mosaics

c. 6th century AD

In Sultanahmet, the floor mosaics of a colonnaded courtyard of the Great Palace of the Byzantine emperors are displayed where they were found. Lively scenes of hunting, wild-animal fights, shepherds and children at play make them some of the most striking works of Byzantine art. The museum is in the Arasta Bazaar behind the Blue Mosque.

Where: Büyük Saray Mozaikleri Müzesi · İstanbul

📷 Dosseman · CC BY-SA 4.0

Tomb of Rumi and the Green Dome

13th century onwards

The tomb of Jalal al-Din Rumi lies beneath the green-tiled dome (Kubbe-i Hadra) that is the symbol of Konya. The mausoleum displays cenotaphs draped in embroidered covers, precious manuscripts of the Masnavi, and Mevlevi musical instruments and garments. Every December the Şeb-i Arus ceremonies draw visitors from around the world.

Where: Mevlana Müzesi · Konya

Sphinxes of Hattusa

Hittite Empire period

Of the two sphinxes that guarded the Sphinx Gate of the Hittite capital Hattusa, one was taken to Germany for restoration in 1917 and returned to Türkiye in 2011; the other was brought from Istanbul, and the pair are now displayed side by side again in the Boğazköy Museum. Carved from limestone, they are among the finest examples of Hittite monumental art.

Where: Boğazköy Müzesi · Çorum
