Hittites and Phrygians

Routes › Hittites and Phrygians

From Hattusa, capital of Anatolia's first great empire, to King Midas's Gordion – with the unique collection of the Museum of Anatolian Civilisations.

🧭 Get directions

Day 1

1 · Ankara · MuseumAnadolu Medeniyetleri Müzesi

The Museum of Anatolian Civilizations sits at the foot of Ankara Castle in the restored 15th-century Mahmut Pasha Bazaar and Kurşunlu Han.

↓ ~1 km (straight line)
2 · Ankara · Archaeological siteRoma Hamamı Örenyeri

The Roman Baths of Ankara are the remains of a large bath complex built in the early 3rd century AD under Emperor Caracalla, when the city was called Ankyra.

Day 2

3 · Çorum · Archaeological siteHattuşa

Hattusa was the capital of the Hittite Empire, a great Bronze Age power, and for some five hundred years governed a state stretching from Anatolia to Syria.

↓ ~1 km (straight line)
4 · Çorum · MuseumBoğazköy Müzesi

Boğazköy Museum, close to the Hattusa site, displays finds from the Hittite capital.

↓ ~24 km (straight line)
5 · Çorum · Archaeological siteAlacahöyük Müzesi ve Örenyeri

Alacahöyük, near the Hittite capital Hattusa, is one of Anatolia's most important Bronze Age centres.

↓ ~42 km (straight line)
6 · Çorum · MuseumÇorum Müzesi

Çorum Museum lies in the heartland of Hittite civilisation and presents finds from major Hittite centres such as Hattusa, Alacahöyük, Şapinuva and Ortaköy.

Day 3

7 · Ankara · MuseumGordion Müzesi

Gordion Museum lies in Yassıhöyük village near Polatlı, beside Gordion, capital of the Phrygian kingdom – the city of legendary King Midas and of the 'Gordian knot'…

↓ ~121 km (straight line)
8 · Eskişehir · Archaeological siteMidas Yazılıkaya Örenyeri

Midas City (Yazılıkaya), in the Han district of Eskişehir at the heart of the Phrygian Valley, is a rock settlement with some of the most impressive Phrygian monuments.
