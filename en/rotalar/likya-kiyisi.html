The Lycian coast: Fethiye to Antalya

Routes › The Lycian coast: Fethiye to Antalya

Rock tombs, cities of the Lycian League and turquoise bays. Starting in Fethiye and heading east along the coast to Antalya, this route is best enjoyed over three days.

🧭 Get directions

Day 1

1 · Muğla · Archaeological siteAmintas Kaya Mezarı

The Rock Tomb of Amyntas, carved into the cliffs above central Fethiye, is one of Lycia's most impressive rock tombs.

2 · Muğla · Archaeological siteFethiye Kayaköy Örenyeri

Kayaköy, near Fethiye, is an abandoned town of hundreds of stone houses spread over the slopes.

4 · Muğla · Archaeological siteFethiye Pınara Örenyeri

Pınara, near Fethiye, is a Lycian city at the foot of a huge rock face riddled with hundreds of tomb openings.

Day 2

5 · Muğla · Archaeological siteFethiye Letoon Örenyeri

Letoon, near Xanthos, was the most important sanctuary of the Lycian League.

↓ ~4 km (straight line)
6 · Antalya · Archaeological siteXanthos Örenyeri

Xanthos was Lycia's most important city and long its administrative centre.

↓ ~10 km (straight line)
7 · Antalya · Archaeological sitePatara Örenyeri

Patara was one of Lycia's most important ports and lies just behind its famous dune-backed beach, which stretches for kilometres.

↓ ~58 km (straight line)

Myra is one of the most striking cities of Lycia, famous for tombs carved into the cliffs at the edge of the Demre plain.
